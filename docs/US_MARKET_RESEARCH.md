# US market research

Evidence snapshot: **2026-09-24**

This document records current public-market observations that can inform strategy. Prices, availability, marketplace result counts and retailer copy are volatile; recheck them before making launch decisions.

## Executive read

The current US-facing market supports **Sauna Whisk** as a clear primary English product term while also using **venik**, **vihta/vasta**, **sauna broom** and **bath broom** around it.

Current specialist-retail examples observed in this snapshot cluster from about **US$14.90 to US$28 for individual whisks**, while marketplace listings are much wider. That range is a market anchor, not a pricing decision: SaunaWhisks.com still needs landed-cost, shipping, margin and acquisition economics before finalizing price.

Latvian origin is already marketed explicitly by a US sauna specialist for birch and oak whisks. That supports Latvia as a potentially understandable commercial differentiator, but our own products must not use Latvian origin in SKU names or claims until supplier-level evidence is complete.

## Marketplace observations

The Sauna Whisk marketplace page showed **150+ items** in the snapshot. Visible examples mixed terms such as **whisk**, **broom**, **venik**, **vihta** and **vasta**, sometimes all inside one marketplace title.

Visible examples included:

- oak whisk/broom/venik at US$13.50 sale price
- a two-pack birch sauna broom at US$33.44
- an aromatic banya venik / vihta / vasta / sauna whisk starting at US$12
- a birch/linden/oak mixed-title listing at US$20
- an oak sauna whisk at US$60 sale price

### Interpretation

Marketplace titles reward synonym coverage and can look heavily keyword-stuffed. That is useful evidence for SEO vocabulary, not a model for SaunaWhisks.com product naming.

Our product pages should stay concise and human:
- Birch Sauna Whisk
- Oak Sauna Whisk
- Eucalyptus Sauna Whisk

Secondary terms belong in explanatory copy, glossary entries, structured educational content and search metadata where natural.

## Assortment and adjacency evidence

The broader category is commercially larger than whisks alone.

FinnStyle's Rento collection combines whisks with:
- sauna scents
- soap / body care
- sauna honey
- buckets and ladles
- brushes
- sauna hats
- towels
- pillows
- diffusers and other accessories

SUPER VENIK explicitly positions itself as a US-based sauna and bath supplier and lists:
- sauna whisks / bath brooms
- bath sets
- herbal bundles
- essential oils
- textiles and clothing
- mattresses and pillows

Its wholesale page says it serves retailers, bathhouses, saunas, spas and other businesses, with volume-based pricing prepared individually.

This supports the strategy of using whisks as the hero category while expanding carefully into adjacent ritual products rather than treating SaunaWhisks.com as a permanently single-SKU-category store.

## Origin positioning

The Sauna Place currently describes its birch product as made from dried Latvian birch branches and its oak product as crafted in Latvia.

Strategic implication:
- **Latvian** can be a meaningful differentiator in US retail language.
- It should become part of our product naming only after the exact commercial SKU has verified origin and supplier evidence.
- Until then, keep origin in verification/status fields rather than product names.

## Claims discipline

Several competitor pages use health or physiological claims around circulation, respiratory effects, pain, cleansing and similar benefits.

Do **not** copy competitor health claims into SaunaWhisks.com merely because they are common in the category.

Our stricter position remains:
- describe aroma, material, preparation, sensory character and cultural use;
- distinguish documented traditional practice from medical efficacy;
- require evidence for health/therapeutic claims;
- avoid detox language.

## Pricing implications

Observed specialist single-whisk examples in this snapshot:
- lower specialist anchor: **US$14.90**
- common specialty example: **US$20**
- branded Rento examples: **US$26–28**
- marketplace: materially wider, with selected visible examples from **US$12 to US$60** before considering packs/bundles

Current SaunaWhisks.com planned single prices (US$24 / US$28 / US$29) sit near the upper half of the observed specialist range.

That does **not** validate those prices. Final pricing must still be derived from:
- landed unit cost
- packaging
- import/brokerage
- fulfilment
- outbound shipping economics
- payment fees
- replacement/damage allowance
- customer acquisition cost
- desired contribution margin
- bundle mix and repeat rate

A premium above low-price specialist competitors needs a visible reason such as verified provenance, product consistency, stronger documentation, better packaging, preparation guidance, bundle economics or service.

## Bundle implications

The market evidence supports a comparison-led bundle because:
- birch and eucalyptus already sell as separate dried products at specialist retailers;
- retailers carry multiple plant materials;
- marketplace sellers frequently offer multi-item packs;
- the category needs education about material differences.

Keep the Discovery Trio as a learning/comparison proposition rather than simply discounting three units.

Before launch, test:
- bundle landed cost
- dimensional shipping weight
- packaging that protects leaves
- discount vs three singles
- gift potential
- first-order conversion
- repeat purchase behavior by material

## Wholesale implications

SUPER VENIK publicly offers tailored volume discounts to retailers, saunas, bathhouses and spas rather than publishing a universal wholesale price.

That supports SaunaWhisks.com's current trade-first enquiry model while actual case economics are still being validated.

Before publishing wholesale tiers, establish:
- case quantities
- minimum order value
- per-case shipping assumptions
- venue consumption rate
- replacement/damage allowance
- target trade gross margin
- retailer margin where relevant
- reorder cadence

## Research gaps

Still required before final US launch strategy:

1. Amazon assortment: current search results were not reliably retrievable in this research pass, so no Amazon price/count assumptions are recorded here.
2. Customer-review coding across multiple retailers: leaf loss, aroma, size, reuse, preparation difficulty, packaging damage and value.
3. Shipping-cost comparison for one whisk vs bundles.
4. Subscription/replenishment evidence: real repeat interval, storage life and seasonal demand.
5. Wholesale case economics and expected retailer margin.
6. SKU-specific landed cost from Latvia to the US.
7. USDA APHIS/import requirements by exact material, condition, treatment and country of origin.
8. Packaging tests for leaf retention and dimensional weight.
9. Current competitor paid-search / organic-search visibility where measurable.
10. Recheck all volatile marketplace counts and prices close to launch.

## Decision rules

- Use competitor prices as context, never as the pricing formula.
- Keep **Sauna Whisk** primary in product naming.
- Use **sauna broom / venik / vihta / vasta** to capture vocabulary without keyword stuffing.
- Treat verified Latvian origin as differentiation, not an assumption.
- Build authority content around material choice, preparation, use, storage and terminology.
- Expand assortment only when each adjacency passes sourcing, compliance, margin and brand-fit review.
- Do not loosen claims standards to match competitor marketing.
